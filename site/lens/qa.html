QA

Targeted assurance

“Where do we point scarce review attention?”

The problem

AI ships more code than any team can humanly review. The economics of QA broke: the queue grows faster than the reviewers, and reviewing everything a little is worse than reviewing the right things properly. Sampling at random is what most teams quietly do now.

What Fieldcraft does

Fieldcraft tells QA where to look. Every run carries signals — iterations burned, criteria graded conservatively, policy near-misses, files with history — that route attention to the changes that earned it. Then it closes the loop: what QA finds becomes a gate check that runs automatically from then on.

Key capabilities

  • Risk-based routing — rank every change by how likely it is to be wrong.
  • “Probably fine” signals — and the evidence for why, so skipping is a decision, not a hope.
  • Build provenance — for anything under review, how it was actually produced.
  • Coverage of the unknowns — what nobody has looked at yet, made visible.
  • Findings become gate checks — a bug found once is a rule enforced forever.

QA stops reviewing everything and starts directing scrutiny. The role shifts from last line of defence to co-author of the standard — which is the only version of QA that survives contact with AI-scale output.
