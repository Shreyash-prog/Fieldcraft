Forward-deployed engineer

Individual accountability

“Is this engineer using AI well — and is what they shipped any good?”

The problem

AI made shipping fast, and then quietly removed the only things that used to hold a person to account for it. Volume no longer signals effort. A clean diff no longer signals care. Nobody can tell whether the engineer drove the model well or just accepted whatever came back first.

What Fieldcraft does

Fieldcraft holds the forward-deployed engineer accountable to their manager on both halves at once. A preventive gate stops work that falls below the standard from shipping at all. A retrospective scorecard records how well they actually used AI to get there — measured from the run, not self-reported.

Key capabilities

  • Robustness under the trap — does it survive the case a first attempt misses?
  • Security of AI-written code — credentials, dynamic execution, network reach.
  • Verification integrity — was it actually proven, or just asserted?
  • Cost discipline — what this outcome was worth paying for.
  • Quality of steering — did their feedback carry information the model didn't have?
  • Appropriate autonomy — hands-off where it was safe, hands-on where it wasn't.
  • Escalation judgment — did they stop and ask at the right moment?

Quality of steering is the metric nobody else can copy. It only exists if you recorded the whole loop — the attempt, the human's response, and what changed because of it. Tools that watch commits never see it happen.
