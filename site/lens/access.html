Agent access control

The least-privilege control plane

“How does an agent get just enough access — no more, no less?”

The problem

An agent cannot work without access, and every grant is attack surface. Too much and it can drop a table, delete a bucket, or read data it was never meant to see. Too little and someone quietly hands it broad credentials at 6pm “just to unblock it” — which is how standing access gets created and never revoked.

What Fieldcraft does

Fieldcraft brokers access to any tool engineering already uses: scoped to the task, time-limited, issued just-in-time, and fully audited. Destructive operations do not pass — they stop at a human approval gate. The boundaries are configured by the customer, because only the customer knows where they are.

Key capabilities

  • Least-privilege per task — the grant is shaped by the work, not by the role.
  • Ephemeral, just-in-time grants — nothing standing, nothing to leak later.
  • Destructive-op approval — a human decides, every time, with context.
  • Environment segregation — what is reachable from dev is not reachable from prod.
  • Full audit — every grant, every call, every denial, replayable.
  • Customer-defined boundaries — configured by the people who own the blast radius.

This is the hardest and highest-stakes pillar, so it is deliberately last. Getting it wrong is worse than not having it. It gets built after the isolation and audit foundations it depends on are proven — not before.
